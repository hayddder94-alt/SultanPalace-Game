# STORY BIBLE

**Project:** The Betrayed Will / وصية الغدر  
**Status:** Locked for planning  
**Playtime the story must fill:** 6–8 hours, not more  
**Assumptions this file depends on:** `00_ASSUMPTIONS_AND_GAPS.md`

---

## 1. Logline

When a dying patriarch names the second son heir, the house splits — and the loyal son who uncovers the conspiracy will not live to see the heir come home.

## 2. Theme

**A will is only words.** Blood does not make a brother. Power does not make a successor. The true inheritance is the thing someone is willing to die to put in your hand.

Secondary themes, kept quiet:

- The eldest son’s wound is real, and still does not excuse him.
- A beautiful house can be a crime scene.
- Truth, once spoken, does not repair what it reveals.

The game is a tragedy with agency, not a nihilistic walk. The player can change *how* the house ends. The player cannot make it innocent again.

## 3. Tone

Intimate political tragedy. Heat and silence. Violence is sudden and personal, never choreographed as spectacle for its own sake. Humor is dry and rare (usually Soren or a servant). The camera stays close to faces.

Reference tone, not reference scale: *The Godfather* (family vs. institution), *A Plague Tale* (authored path, cruelty, tenderness), *Macbeth* (usurpation and the thing you cannot stop), Neo-Babylonian visual world.

## 4. Dramatic question

**Will Raynor take back the house, or take apart the lie the house was built on?**

Evan’s dramatic question, which occupies the first two acts: **Can a discarded son make the family tell the truth before it kills him?**

The answer to Evan’s question is no. That is the point. His death is the argument. Raynor’s act is the reply.

## 5. The will, literally

### The public will (Prologue)

Read by a temple witness in the audience hall, before the six sons, Leila, and a small court of household officers.

1. The riverside palace and all household staff pass to Raynor.
2. The eastern caravan-gate charter passes to Raynor.
3. The irrigation concession of the two southern districts passes to Raynor.
4. Each other son is granted a stipend and a room in the house for life, contingent on acknowledging the heir.
5. Leila’s betrothal to Raynor is confirmed as Orin’s last political act.
6. Nofan is named *advisor to the heir*, a public honor that is also a public humiliation.

### The second tablet (hidden)

Sealed with Orin’s private mark and left with High Priest Bel-Iddin.

If Raynor dies within one year of the reading, the charter returns to the crown and the irrigation concession is frozen until the temple and the court appoint a steward. The tablet exists to make assassination expensive. It is why Raynor is hidden, not buried.

Evan spends Act I chasing the *idea* of a second writing. Raynor spends Act III holding it.

## 6. The conspiracy, as it actually happened

This is the truth the player reconstructs. It is not dumped in a single speech.

1. Nofan learns, two nights before Orin’s death, that the will names Raynor. He goes to Darius and Malik.
2. They agree Raynor cannot be killed. The second tablet is rumored, not yet found.
3. On the night after the reading, Darius’s trusted guards intercept Raynor on the service canal under the cover of an “escort to the northern estates.”
4. Raynor is taken to **Kurnah Hold**, an old family fortress written off the books a decade earlier. Malik erased it from the ledgers.
5. The official story: Raynor left at dawn to inspect the estates. A letter in his hand (forged by Malik) is shown to the household.
6. Lord Hazar, Nofan’s court patron, agrees to keep the King uninterested for a season.
7. Leila is told the official story. She does not believe it. She also does not yet know where Raynor is. She begins her own quiet search, which is why Evan cannot read her.
8. Evan asks the wrong questions. Malik forges a note from Soren. Evan is ambushed at the river storehouse, stabbed, thrown into the canal, and announced drowned.
9. Darius exceeded the plan. Nofan wanted Evan frightened, not dead. Nofan does not punish Darius. That is the moment the conspiracy can no longer be walked back.
10. Evan survives, finds Kurnah Hold, frees Raynor, and dies at the inner gate.
11. Raynor returns to a city that has already started to believe a different history.

## 7. Structure

Nine blocks. Do not add a tenth.

Times are median play, including short cinematics.

### PROLOGUE — The Last Night (18 min) — Playable: Raynor

Orin is dying in the upper chamber. Raynor is summoned. A short playable walk through the sleeping palace: servants, a prayer niche, the sound of the river. Last conversation with Orin. Orin admits he is afraid of Nofan’s hunger and of Raynor’s mercy. He asks Raynor to protect Evan and Soren specifically.

Cinematic: dawn, the reading of the will. Nofan’s face. Darius’s stillness. Malik already writing. Soren looking at Evan. Leila does not smile. Raynor accepts the seal.

Private scene: Leila and Raynor on the terrace. She says the temple will honor the betrothal. She also says, “If they loved him, they would have been kinder in their eyes.”

The prologue ends on Raynor walking toward the canal gate at dusk. Fade.

### CHAPTER 1 — The Named Son (40 min) — Playable: Evan

Morning. The player is Evan and does not know where Raynor is. The house is performing joy. Evan can walk the family wing, the kitchen yard, the archive anteroom. Conversations with Soren (uneasy), Malik (too helpful), a servant who will not meet his eyes.

First investigation verbs come online. Evan finds Raynor’s chamber unused — the bed not slept in, the scar-oil still on the table. A boatman at the service dock says a covered boat left before dawn.

Nofan asks Evan, kindly, to stop making the servants nervous.

Chapter close: Evan finds a bronze clasp from Raynor’s cloak in the canal mud.

### CHAPTER 2 — The Empty Seat (45 min) — Playable: Evan

The household holds a public breakfast with an empty heir’s chair. Evan is told to sit and be quiet. He does not.

He follows the clasp to the lower docks and the residential lanes behind the palace. He speaks to a reed merchant, a night guard who was rotated off the canal that night, and Leila at the temple gate. Leila gives him nothing free. She asks what he is prepared to lose.

First stealth space: the guard barracks annex, to read the duty roster Darius altered.

First combat: if detected, two guards in a storage court. Non-lethal is allowed here; lethal is not yet the game’s language.

Chapter close: the forged letter is real paper with the wrong seal-pressure. Evan knows Raynor did not write it.

### CHAPTER 3 — The House Lies (50 min) — Playable: Evan

Investigation expands into the market and the temple archive. Evan reconstructs a deleted ledger line: a fortress, *Kurnah*, still drawing grain once a month.

He can confront Malik and be stonewalled. He can ask Soren and watch Soren almost break. He can take the grain line to Leila, who recognizes the old name and goes still.

Combat unlock: house sickle-sword, after a street confrontation with men who are not city watch and not house guard — Hazar’s, though Evan cannot know that yet.

Chapter close: Evan pins three names on the board — Darius (roster), Malik (ledger), Nofan (the only man the other two would move for). He still does not know where Kurnah is.

### CHAPTER 4 — The Canal (40 min) — Playable: Evan

A note in Soren’s hand: *the river storehouse, after the third watch, I know where he is.*

Evan can check Soren’s room. Soren is not there. The handwriting is almost right.

The storehouse is a trap. Playable infiltration that becomes an ambush. Darius is present but does not swing the knife himself. A trusted captain does. Evan is stabbed, dumped, and the world goes black in the water.

Cinematic: Nofan is told. He is furious, then very calm. He instructs Malik to write a drowning. He lights a lamp in Evan’s room as if in mourning.

Chapter close: a reed cutter’s hands. Coughing. Night. Evan is alive and no one in the house knows.

### CHAPTER 5 — The Man Who Drowned (45 min) — Playable: Evan

Evan cannot go home. The residential district and the temple back courts become his map. He is believed dead; NPCs speak of him in the past tense. This is the environmental-storytelling chapter.

He finds Soren in a shrine, wrecked with guilt — Soren’s seal-ring was stolen the night of the note. Soren is not the author. Soren knows a story about Kurnah from childhood: the place their grandfather put men “until the river forgot them.”

Leila finds Evan before the guards do. She has decided he is more useful alive. She gives him the old survey mark for Kurnah Hold and a temple token that will open its water gate once.

Chapter close: Evan on a reed boat at dusk, looking at a black fortress in the marsh.

### CHAPTER 6 — The Gate (50 min) — Playable: Evan, then Raynor for the last minutes

Infiltration of Kurnah Hold. This is the Vertical Slice’s older, harder brother, and the emotional peak of Evan’s game.

Stealth through the reed wall and the lower cisterns. Investigation: Raynor’s cell wall, the food ledger, a letter from Nofan that says *keep him unbroken*. Combat as the alert rises.

Evan reaches Raynor. Playable, not only cinematic: unlock, shoulder, first conversation in the dark. Raynor is thinner, scar deeper, furious at Evan for coming, then grateful in the way men like him get grateful — by standing up.

Escape. The inner gate. Darius’s reinforcements. Evan tells Raynor to go and does not make it a discussion.

Playable last stand. Short. Brutal. A spear. The house seal pressed into Raynor’s hand.

Cinematic: Raynor on the marsh path with a dying man’s blood on the seal. He looks back once. He does not get to stay.

Control transfers. The next input the player makes is Raynor’s.

### CHAPTER 7 — The Returned (70 min) — Playable: Raynor

Same city, night-state, heavier body.

Raynor cannot walk in the front gate. He uses Evan’s map. He visits the places Evan died trying to understand, and they read differently now.

He confronts Soren (ally, if the player did not treat him as a coward in Act I — this is a flag, not a morality meter). He confronts Leila (the betrothal is still legally real; the trust is not). He can spare or kill the captain who stabbed Evan.

Darius will not be talked down. Darius is a combat confrontation in the training court at night.

Malik tries to bargain with ledgers. Malik can be turned over to the temple or left in his archive with the door barred. Killing Malik is allowed and costs the cleanest ending flag.

Nofan is not yet available. He is at court with Hazar.

Chapter close: Leila brings the rumor of the second tablet’s location — or, if the player has treated her as an enemy, Soren does, less completely.

### CHAPTER 8 — The Second Tablet (75 min) — Playable: Raynor

Revelations land in play, not in a lecture.

- The second tablet is recovered from the temple vault or from Hazar’s townhouse, depending on Leila’s flag.
- Hazar is unmasked as the court weight behind Nofan. If Hazar is on-screen (scope permitting), he is a conversation boss, not a sword boss. If not, his letters are enough.
- Nofan returns to the audience hall — the same room as the prologue — and waits. He knew Raynor would come there.

Final confrontation is staged, not a 20-minute health bar. Dialogue first, with the will physically present. Combat only if the player refuses Nofan’s last bargain (exile for both, house given to the temple). Most players should be allowed to end Nofan without a long fight — a single decisive exchange — or to spare him and make him read Evan’s name into the record.

Leila’s last scene is the other half of the ending. She either stands with Raynor, or she takes the tablet back to the temple and leaves him the house but not her.

Epilogue cards, 90–120 seconds. The irrigation gates open or do not. Soren stays or leaves. The empty chair is filled or burned.

No post-credits teaser for a sequel. The story ends.

## 8. Endings (three states, one path)

Flags that matter, and only these:

1. **NofanAlive** (spare / kill / bind-to-record)
2. **LeilaTrust** (earned / broken)
3. **MalikExposed** (public / dead / fled) — modifies epilogue only

| State | Condition | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| *The House Stands* | Nofan spared or bound, LeilaTrust earned, Malik exposed | Raynor holds the house. The will is read again, with Evan’s name. Cost is visible. |
| *The House is a Name* | Mixed flags | Raynor holds power or exile; someone he needed is gone. Bittersweet default. |
| *The House is Ash* | Nofan killed in rage + LeilaTrust broken + Malik dead | Raynor wins the building and loses the meaning. Last shot is the empty terrace. |

No fourth ending. No “secret good” ending that requires collectibles.

## 9. Character engines (story function only)

| Character | Engine | If removed, the story breaks because… |
|---|---|---|
| Orin | The will, the standard, the absence | There is no crime and no measure |
| Raynor | The chosen, the second body, the question | There is no second act identity |
| Evan | The eyes, the death, the moral argument | There is no investigation and no cost |
| Nofan | The wound, the plan, the last room | There is no antagonist with a face |
| Leila | The unreadability, the tablet, the non-blood stake | The story becomes only men fighting over a house |
| Darius | The violence of the plan | The abduction and the last stand have no author |
| Malik | The paper of the plan | The investigation has no opponent |
| Soren | The conscience of the house | There is no brother the player can still save |
| Hazar | The world outside the family | Optional; the story still works as a closed house tragedy |

## 10. What the story will not do

- Reveal that Raynor is not Orin’s son.
- Reveal that Evan is the secret heir.
- Make Leila Evan’s secret lover as a twist.
- Redeem Nofan into a co-op partner.
- Resurrect Evan.
- End on a sequel hook about a greater evil under the ziggurat.
- Turn the second tablet into a magic artifact.
- Require the player to kill every brother.

## 11. Motifs to repeat (so reuse feels like design)

- Empty chairs
- Wrong seal-pressure on wax
- Canal water (birth of the plot, Evan’s false death, the path to Kurnah)
- Oil lamps lit for the living and the dead
- Hands: Orin’s, Evan’s (the seal), Raynor’s (the scar), Nofan’s (always clean)
- The audience hall, returned to in Prologue, Ch 1, Ch 7/8

## 12. Language and presentation of text

- Spoken VO may be English placeholder through Vertical Slice.
- All player-facing narrative text is written so it can be subtitled in English and Arabic without cultural inversion.
- Tablets and ledgers are short. No one wants to read three pages in a 7-hour game.
- Evan’s evidence notes are in his voice. Raynor’s later notes are shorter, angrier, more precise.

## 13. Narrative production rules

1. If a scene does not change a relationship or a fact, cut it.
2. If a character explains what the camera can show, cut the line.
3. Cinematics longer than 4 minutes need a director exception.
4. Evan’s death is playable. Do not steal the player’s last inputs.
5. The control transfer to Raynor happens in the same session, no chapter-select jump.
6. Flashbacks are limited to two optional memory rooms and one forced Orin memory in Ch 8. No flashback campaign.

## 14. Title, twice

**The Betrayed Will** is Orin’s document, violated by his sons.  
It is also Evan’s last will — not written, pressed into a hand.

Arabic **وصية الغدر** carries the same double edge: the testament, and the treachery inside it. Keep both meanings visible. Do not subtitle them away.
