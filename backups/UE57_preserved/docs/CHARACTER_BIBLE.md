# CHARACTER BIBLE

**Project:** The Betrayed Will / وصية الغدر  
**Status:** Locked for planning  
**Priority order for production quality:** Raynor → Leila → Evan → Nofan → supporting

Face, silhouette, voice, and written behaviour are the budget. We would rather have eight memorable people than forty extras with names.

---

## 1. House Orin at a glance

Patriarch: **Orin** (deceased as the game begins; alive only in the prologue and memory).

Sons, birth order:

1. Nofan — eldest, passed over
2. Raynor — chosen heir, second playable
3. Darius — commander of the house guard
4. Evan — first playable
5. Malik — steward
6. Soren — youngest

Non-blood primary: **Leila**, daughter of High Priest Bel-Iddin, betrothed to Raynor.

Epithets below are ceremonial. Player-facing names stay as written in the brief.

## 2. Playable characters

### 2.1 Raynor

| Field | Lock |
|---|---|
| Epithet | Raynor *Sha-Re’u* — “he who watches the flock” |
| Age | 34 |
| Role | Chosen heir. Victim of the abduction. Playable in Prologue and Chapters 7–8 |
| Height / build | Tall, broad, a fighter’s posture even when still |
| Distinguishing mark | A pale, clean scar from left brow to cheekbone, from a border skirmish over an irrigation cut. Visible in every close-up. Not a disfigurement. A signature. |
| Movement | Heavy, economical, shoulders first |
| Combat identity | Longer reach, charge-heavy, short finisher window. Less quiet than Evan. |
| Voice direction | Low, precise, little decoration. Anger is compressed, not shouted, until Ch 8. |
| Want | To keep the house from becoming Nofan’s idea of a house |
| Need | To accept that winning the house is not the same as deserving Evan’s death |
| Fear | That Orin was wrong — that mercy is how houses die |
| Relationship to Leila | Respect, unfinished love, political fact. He does not own her and knows it. |
| Relationship to Evan | Protective, slightly dismissive at first, then the center of his grief |
| Relationship to Nofan | Older-brother friction that calcifies into a single room at the end |
| Design notes | Charismatic without being charming. Serious without being grimdark. Capable fighter — the player must *feel* the upgrade when control transfers. |
| Must never | Monologue about honor. Joke after Evan dies. Become a silent tank. |

**Silhouette:** cloak-weight at the shoulders, scar readable at medium shot, khopesh on the left hip after Ch 7.

**Performance key:** “I was built to carry a house. I was not built to bury a brother who came for me.”

### 2.2 Evan

| Field | Lock |
|---|---|
| Epithet | Evan *Sha-Shemi* — “he who hears” |
| Age | 27 |
| Role | First long playable. Investigator. Moral argument of the game. Dies at the end of Ch 6. |
| Height / build | Shorter than Raynor, lean, quick |
| Distinguishing mark | Ink-stained fingers. A cheap river-stone bead on his wrist that belonged to their mother. |
| Movement | Light, curious, head turns before feet |
| Combat identity | Shorter range, better stealth, weaker poise. Wins by reading, not by dominating. |
| Voice direction | Warm, questioning, not naive. He notices before he accuses. |
| Want | To bring Raynor home and make the family stop lying |
| Need | To act, not only observe — he completes this need at the gate |
| Fear | That he does not matter enough for anyone to tell him the truth |
| Relationship to Raynor | Loyalty without worship |
| Relationship to Leila | He cannot read her and hates that he needs her |
| Relationship to Soren | Protective older-middle. The only brother he fully trusts at the start. |
| Relationship to Malik / Nofan | Habit of courtesy that becomes a weapon used against him |
| Design notes | Investigative, loyal, resourceful, initially unaware of the full conspiracy — as the brief requires. The player should like him enough that Ch 6 hurts. |
| Must never | Become comic relief. Survive via retcon. Turn into a second Raynor in combat. |

**Silhouette:** open collar, no heroic armor, a satchel, the wrist bead.

**Performance key:** “If I stop asking, I become them.”

## 3. Primary non-playable

### 3.1 Leila

| Field | Lock |
|---|---|
| Epithet | Leila *Marat-Ishtar* in temple context; Leila of the Gate in the house |
| Age | 29 |
| Role | Political and emotional hinge. Holder of access to the second tablet. Not a blood relative. |
| Origin | Daughter of High Priest Bel-Iddin. Raised in temple courts, not in House Orin. |
| Height / build | Tall for the cast, composed, unhurried |
| Distinguishing mark | A thin gold temple-line painted from the inner eye at ceremony; a practical unpainted face when she does not want to be seen as the temple. |
| Voice direction | Intelligent, dry, never breathless. She asks questions that rearrange the room. |
| Want | The temple’s independence from both House Orin and the court |
| Need | To decide whether love is a political error |
| Fear | Being used as a seal on a man’s document |
| Alignment | Mysterious first. Capable of withholding. Capable of saving. Not a simple villain, not a prize. |
| Relationship to Raynor | Betrothed by Orin. Real feeling, real calculation. Both are true. |
| Relationship to Evan | She uses him. She also keeps him alive in Ch 5. The player must be allowed to resent her and thank her. |
| Relationship to Nofan | He has approached her. She has not joined him. He believes she might. |
| Design notes | Attractive, intelligent, charismatic, as specified. Attractiveness is carriage and attention, not a camera that leers. Costume is readable at silhouette: temple white-gold vs. city clay-dark when she moves unseen. |
| Must never | Be reduced to a twist-revealed mastermind of the entire plot. Be reduced to “the girlfriend.” Be sexually displayed as a reward. |

**Performance key:** “I will not set fire to my temple to keep your house warm.”

**Player-readable states:**

- *Closed* — default, Prologue through Ch 3
- *Allied-for-now* — Ch 5 token, Ch 7 if treated with respect
- *Withdrawn* — if Raynor kills Malik in rage or treats the tablet as a weapon against the temple

These are flags, not a romance meter.

### 3.2 Nofan

| Field | Lock |
|---|---|
| Epithet | Nofan *Aplu* — the eldest, said with a little too much care |
| Age | 37 |
| Role | Architect of the conspiracy. Final human antagonist. |
| Height / build | As tall as Raynor, narrower, always dressed for a room with witnesses |
| Distinguishing mark | Clean hands. A habit of aligning objects on a table before he speaks. |
| Voice direction | Warm when he is most dangerous. He rarely raises his voice. He is a host. |
| Want | To inherit what he spent his life preparing to run |
| Need | To believe Orin was mistaken, because the alternative is that he was unseen |
| Fear | Being the son who did all the work and is remembered as a thief |
| Motive (believable) | He did the court work Raynor refused. He kept Hazar fed and the charter intact. He thinks Raynor’s honesty will get the irrigation concession stripped and the household sold. He is wrong about the method and not entirely wrong about the danger. |
| What he will not do (at first) | Order Evan killed. Kill Raynor. Burn the house. |
| What he does anyway | Lets Darius remain after Evan’s “drowning.” That is his mortal sin. |
| Relationship to Orin | Love, humiliation, unfinished argument |
| Relationship to Raynor | A lifetime of comparison |
| Relationship to Darius / Malik | He is their brain. He is not their master once blood is down. |
| Design notes | Must not be a simplistic villain. Give him one scene in Ch 1 where he is genuinely kind to a servant, and one scene in Ch 8 where he is still sure he was the responsible one. |
| Must never | Cackle. Reveal a childhood torture origin. Beg in a way that erases him. |

**Performance key:** “I was the will before he wrote it down.”

### 3.3 Orin

| Field | Lock |
|---|---|
| Age | 71 |
| Role | Dead sun. Appears alive only in the prologue and in at most two memories. |
| Body | A man who was large, now collapsing inward. Still has a voice that fills a room. |
| Voice direction | Tired, specific, no mysticism. He talks about water and sons, not destiny. |
| Want | A house that will outlive his worst son and his best one |
| Need | To admit he made Nofan and then looked away |
| Design notes | Do not cast him as a saint. He played his sons against each other for years because it kept them sharp. The will is an attempt to stop a machine he built. |
| Must never | Appear as a ghost giving quests. |

## 4. Supporting brothers

### 4.1 Darius

Age 31. House guard commander. A soldier who likes orders more than questions. Joined the conspiracy because Nofan framed it as protecting the house from a weak heir, and because Raynor would have audited the guard.

He exceeds the plan when he has Evan killed. He will not apologize. He is the cleanest combat antagonist in the game.

Look: cropped hair, leather and bronze, a broken nose. Voice: short sentences.

Must never monologue. His body is the speech.

### 4.2 Malik

Age 26. Steward. Thin, careful, proud of ink. He forged the letter and the drowning notice and deleted Kurnah from the books. He believes paper is the real palace.

He will bargain. He will name Hazar if it buys him a door. He is more afraid than evil, and still responsible.

Look: always one more ring than he needs. Stained cuffs he pretends are clean.

### 4.3 Soren

Age 21. Temple-educated youngest. Not a founder of the plot. His seal-ring is stolen to write the trap note. He is the brother the player can still save.

He is not comic relief and not a child. He is a man who was never invited into the real conversation and hates himself for being relieved.

Look: priest-student wool, too-long sleeves, eyes that give him away.

If treated as a coward repeatedly, he still helps in Ch 7, but the *House Stands* ending becomes unavailable. That is the only “relationship cost” in the game.

## 6. Relationship map (start state)

```
                    ORIN (dead / dying)
                     /    |    \
                 NOFAN  RAYNOR  DARIUS
                   |      |  \     |
                   |    LEILA  \    |
                   |      |     EVAN
                   |      |      |
                 HAZAR   TEMPLE  SOREN
                          |
                        MALIK (paper binds all of them)
```

Pressure lines:

- Nofan ↔ Raynor: succession
- Nofan ↔ Hazar: court oxygen
- Raynor ↔ Leila: betrothal / unreadability
- Evan ↔ Malik: courtesy used as a blade
- Evan ↔ Soren: the last clean bond
- Darius ↔ everyone: the moment words become a knife
- Leila ↔ Bel-Iddin: the tablet she can ask for and may not receive

## 7. Casting / performance brief (placeholder through VS)

Until professional VO:

- Generated or scratch voices are allowed.
- Scratch must still hit the **performance key** of each primary.
- Raynor and Evan must be immediately distinguishable in one spoken line.
- Leila must never be pitched as “seductive default.”
- Nofan must never be pitched as “villain default.”

Final VO pass is a polish-phase item, not a Vertical Slice blocker, except for the will-reading and Evan’s last lines, which should be the best voices we can get even in the slice.

## 8. Visual production priority

Hero sculpts, hero textures, hero eyes:

1. Raynor
2. Leila
3. Evan
4. Nofan

Secondary sculpts can share a body base with unique heads. Orin needs a hero head for the prologue close-ups. Darius needs a readable combat silhouette more than a hero face. Malik and Soren need good faces; their bodies can be simpler.

Costume changes are story-state, not a wardrobe system:

- Evan: day house clothes / night wet clothes after Ch 4 / infiltration wrap in Ch 6
- Raynor: prologue formal / prisoner ruin / returned cloak in Ch 7–8
- Leila: temple / city / final hall

That is the entire costume list for principals.

## 9. What we will not add

- A seventh brother
- A secret sister who is the real heir
- A companion animal
- A romance minigame
- Child versions of the cast beyond one optional memory
- A playable Leila chapter

## 10. Character test (use in reviews)

A character is finished enough to ship when:

1. A player can describe them in one sentence after one scene.
2. They have a want and a need that are not the same.
3. They can be in a quiet scene and still be themselves.
4. Removing them breaks a specific story beat, not just “flavor.”
